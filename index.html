Clinical context — history & vitals (optional, but improves interpretation)

Respiratory

Known COPD or chronic CO₂ retentionHelps distinguish acute vs acute-on-chronic respiratory acidosis
Recent pneumonia or chest infectionSupports V/Q mismatch; may explain a widened A–a gradient
Suspected or confirmed pulmonary embolismClassic cause of unexplained respiratory alkalosis with a widened A–a
Opioid or sedative use in the last 24 hSupports acute respiratory acidosis from hypoventilation
Mechanical ventilation or impending respiratory failureMay explain a normal or near-normal ABG despite severe disease

Gastrointestinal / metabolic

Large-volume diarrhea (ongoing)Classic cause of normal-anion-gap (hyperchloremic) acidosis
Vomiting or nasogastric suctionChloride-responsive metabolic alkalosis
Diuretic use (current or recent)Can cause metabolic alkalosis or a mixed picture
Known diabetes, on insulin, or DKA suspectedSupports high-anion-gap ketoacidosis
Recent cardiac arrest, shock, or severe hypoperfusionSupports high-anion-gap lactic acidosis
Ileostomy, ureteral diversion, or high-output stomaGI bicarbonate loss → normal-anion-gap acidosis
Recent large-volume normal saline resuscitationDilutional hyperchloremic acidosis

Toxicology / renal

Suspected toxic alcohol ingestion (methanol, ethylene glycol, isopropanol)High-AG acidosis with an elevated osmolal gap — an emergency
Salicylate (aspirin) ingestion or overdoseClassic mixed high-AG acidosis + respiratory alkalosis
Known renal failure or on dialysisSupports high-AG acidosis; also raises the baseline anion gap
Known renal tubular acidosis or use of acetazolamide / topiramateDrug-induced or inherited NAGMA
Recent seizure, rhabdomyolysis, or prolonged immobilisationCan raise the anion gap via lactate and organic acids
